Who you're working with

Design that starts with the constraints, not the catalogue.

I'm Nicolas Quiroz. I help startups design and build the physical infrastructure their AI workloads run on — from a single 8-GPU node in a server room to multi-rack deployments with dedicated power and cooling.

Most teams deploying GPUs for the first time budget carefully for the hardware and then discover the hard constraints afterwards: the circuit can't carry the load, the room can't reject the heat, the cables are the wrong length, the switch has the wrong optics. Each of those is a delay measured in weeks and a cost measured in five figures.

I work through that layer with you before anything is ordered, so the build you plan is the build you get.

The approach is deliberately unglamorous: establish the hard limits first — what the room can power, what it can cool, what the budget genuinely covers — and design inside them. It is far cheaper to find a constraint on a drawing than on a loading dock.
