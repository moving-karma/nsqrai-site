Who you're working with

Design that starts with the constraints, not the catalogue.

I'm Nicolas Quiroz. I help startups design and build the physical infrastructure their AI workloads run on — from a single 8-GPU node in a server room to multi-rack deployments with dedicated power and cooling.

Most teams deploying GPUs for the first time budget carefully for the hardware and discover the hard constraints afterwards: the circuit can't carry the load, the room can't reject the heat, the cables are the wrong length, the switch has the wrong optics. Each is a delay measured in weeks and a cost measured in five figures.

The approach is deliberately unglamorous — establish the hard limits first, then design inside them. It is far cheaper to find a constraint on a drawing than on a loading dock.

Typical engagement

A startup with funding for compute, a room or colo cage, and no in-house datacenter engineer. You know the GPUs you want. You need everything between the wall and the workload specified correctly the first time.
